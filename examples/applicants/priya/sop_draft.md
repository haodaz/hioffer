# Statement of purpose — rough draft

I am applying to the PhD program in Computer Science to work on multilingual natural language processing, specifically making large language models useful for the hundreds of millions of people who speak Indian languages but are poorly served by today's models.

My interest comes from a concrete failure. In 2025 I built a Tamil–English code-switched evaluation set of 2,400 sentences and found that two open 7B models lost 18–26 points compared with monolingual Tamil inputs. People in Chennai do not speak monolingual Tamil to a chatbot; they switch. That result became my first-author workshop paper and convinced me that evaluation, not just training, is where multilingual NLP is broken.

At Microsoft Research India in summer 2026 I worked on the other side of the problem: adaptation. Extending a sentencepiece vocabulary with 6k Kannada tokens cut tokens per sentence by 41% and improved QA F1 by 3.4 points at the same fine-tuning budget. The lesson was that cheap, targeted changes to the tokenizer can matter as much as expensive fine-tuning.

In a PhD I want to study (1) parameter-efficient and data-efficient adaptation of LLMs to low-resource languages, (2) evaluation that captures code-switching, cultural grounding and faithfulness, and (3) speech-text models for languages whose data is mostly spoken.

I have a 9.21/10 CGPA, a first-author workshop paper, and experience as a TA for a 120-student machine learning course. I organise my department's NLP reading group.
