# Statement of purpose — rough draft

I study how people who cannot see a screen can still tell when an AI is wrong. In my diary study with 14
blind screen-reader users, the most common strategy for checking an LLM answer was asking a sighted friend,
which is exactly the dependence these tools were supposed to remove. SourceSpeak, the plug-in I built in
response, reads provenance cues aloud; in a within-subjects study participants caught 2.3 times more incorrect
answers. That result is the core of my first-author submission.

At Microsoft Research Asia I looked at a different population with a related problem: older adults using
conversational agents. When a family member initiated the repair after an agent error, continued use over four
weeks doubled. Trust is repaired socially, not just technically.

In a PhD I want to design and evaluate interfaces that make AI provenance and errors legible to people who are
usually left out of the design process, using mixed methods and working prototypes.
