# Statement of purpose — rough draft

I want to build robots that can help in ordinary homes, which means learning manipulation skills from very
little data on hardware that families can afford. My M.Tech work at IISc has been about exactly that gap: with
40 demonstrations per task, a diffusion policy on a $1,800 arm reached 78% on cup stacking and 61% on towel
folding, far above a behaviour-cloning baseline. The towel number is the one I think about most: deformable
objects are where vision alone fails, and where my undergraduate tactile skin (64 taxels, slip detected in 60 ms)
suggests a cheap way forward.

At TCS Research I worked on the other half of the problem, getting simulation to look like a real kitchen:
reconstructing a counter from twelve phone photos cut the sim-to-real success drop from 41 points to 17.

In a PhD I want to combine these threads: few-shot visuomotor learning, low-cost tactile sensing for deformable
objects, and real-to-sim transfer for household tasks, with assistive robotics for elderly care as the setting
that matters most to me.
